Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026. This policy covers the Tracewire app for Android (package uk.jj.tracewire) and this website. Tracewire is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Tracewire watches your phone's connections on the phone itself. What it learns is kept on your phone and never sent to the developer or anyone else.
  • It never decrypts secure connections, and never reads the contents of what apps send.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • The only things it fetches are encrypted DNS answers from the service you choose, and newer lists from this website, monthly, if you allow it.

How Tracewire sees connections

Tracewire uses Android's VPN feature to pass your phone's connections through its own engine, which runs on the phone. It is not a VPN service: your traffic is not sent to any other server, and your internet address does not change. For each connection Tracewire notes the app, the server's address and port, the server's name where the app announces it (in a website look-up, or at the start of a secure connection), how much data went each way, and when.

What stays on your phone

  • Your connection history, kept for as long as you choose (30 days to start with) and then deleted. Daily totals for each app are kept.
  • Your rules, alerts, data plan and settings.
  • Reports, connection lists and packet captures you make. They stay in the app until you share them.

Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for Tracewire's data, so none of it is copied to a Google account. Clear all history in Settings deletes it from the phone.

Encrypted DNS

If encrypted DNS is on (it is, to start with), the website names your apps look up are sent encrypted to the service you choose: Quad9 (Switzerland), Cloudflare or Mullvad (Sweden). That service sees the names and your internet address, under its own privacy policy. If it cannot be reached, the look-up goes to your network's normal DNS, as it would without Tracewire. Choose Phone's normal DNS to switch encryption off.

Lists

The country, network and company lists are part of the app. Once a month, if Check every month is on, Tracewire asks this website whether newer lists exist and downloads them. This site sees an ordinary request, like any visit, and nothing about your connections.

Sharing

A report, list or capture leaves your phone only when you share it, and only to the app you pick. A packet capture holds everything that passed while it was recording, so share it with care.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking. GitHub may keep the usual server logs, such as internet addresses, under its own privacy policy. Downloads come from GitHub's release storage.

Children

Tracewire is not aimed at children and collects nothing about anyone.
